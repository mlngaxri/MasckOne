This ledger gives a non-technical reviewer a fast way to distinguish inspectable project evidence from hypotheses and future validation. It is intentionally conservative. A documented requirement, model, plan or test framework is not treated as proof that a physical product works or that customers want it.

Engineering references should be read against live engineering `main` revision `8d37bc322b5ebe42179685a1a0559f2fcb1b5f22`, verified on 17 September 2026. Scholarship documentation may advance independently and is not a newer engineering release.

## What should not be inferred

The repository does not currently establish customer demand, revenue, partnerships, willingness to pay, population fit, comfort, product safety, hygiene practicality, real fluid performance, manufacturing feasibility, viable unit economics or product-level physical validation.

The broader wearable-and-dock vision is a direction. It should not be read as a committed first-product specification or as evidence that every proposed function will survive customer, physical and commercial testing.

## What happens next

The evidence path is deliberately narrower than the long-term vision:

1. Structured customer interviews to identify a repeated problem and credible first-use-case candidate.
2. Focused simulation and analysis to narrow the physical questions worth testing.
3. A focused physical fit prototype to investigate fit, comfort and workflow assumptions.
4. Core fluid-delivery testing to replace key digital assumptions with measurements.
5. Hygiene and cleaning investigation to test whether servicing burden undermines convenience.
6. Initial manufacturing-cost modelling using sourced assumptions to test commercial plausibility.

Scholarship support would be most useful where it accelerates those evidence steps through prototyping materials, test equipment, manufacturing and cost investigation, and structured customer validation. No funding amount, technical outcome or commercial result is assumed.
