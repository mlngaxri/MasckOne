# Scholarship evidence gaps

This page shows the main evidence I still need before I should treat Masck One as a product candidate rather than an actively developed venture hypothesis. I use it to keep the next stage focused on uncertainty reduction rather than feature accumulation.

## Priority evidence gaps

| Evidence gap | What I know now | What I do not know yet | Next evidence | Decision it should unlock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer problem and first use case | My informal survey of around 20 people gave a preliminary, non-conclusive signal: time/convenience drew the strongest interest, while comfort, maintenance and price were recurring concerns. | I do not know whether one routine task is important and repeated enough to justify changing behaviour or paying for a product. | Structured customer interviews focused on current routines, alternatives, friction, maintenance trade-offs and price sensitivity. | Nominate one first-use-case candidate, narrow the target user or reject the current problem hypothesis. |
| Fit and wearable interaction | I have inspectable digital geometry, requirements and integration work. | I do not know whether the relevant geometry is comfortable, accessible or credible on real people. | Focused analysis followed by a deliberately limited physical fit prototype. | Continue the wearable interaction, change the architecture or reduce scope before broader prototyping. |
| Fluid delivery and recovery | I have digital subsystem architecture and engineering targets. | I do not know whether the minimum fluid interaction for a selected use case works repeatably in physical testing. | Core fluid-delivery and recovery testing under recorded conditions. | Continue, redesign or reject the fluid architecture for the selected use case. |
| Hygiene and cleaning burden | I have identified cleaning and maintenance as both a customer concern and a system constraint. | I do not know whether preparation, cleaning and storage would erase the convenience benefit or create unacceptable hygiene requirements. | Hygiene and cleaning investigation tied to the actual first-use workflow. | Keep the workflow, simplify it, change the architecture or reject the use case. |
| Manufacturing cost and commercial plausibility | I have not claimed a launch price, margin or viable unit economics. | I do not know whether likely manufacturing cost can plausibly overlap with the value and price sensitivity customers describe. | Initial manufacturing-cost modelling using sourced assumptions for the narrowed architecture. | Continue commercial development, simplify scope, redesign cost drivers or stop before broader product development. |

## Why I keep these gaps visible

The repository demonstrates that I have moved beyond an idea into structured digital development, but digital execution does not close the most important venture questions above. I do not treat commit volume, polished renders, AI output, software checks or scholarship expenditure as substitutes for customer or physical evidence.

My long-term direction remains a wearable-and-dock system that could automate selected parts of a facial-skincare routine. I have deliberately not locked the first product use case. I want customer evidence to identify a narrow problem first, then use focused analysis and physical testing to determine whether the relevant interaction is credible, and only then deepen manufacturing and commercial work.

A negative result is useful. If evidence weakens a core assumption, I should narrow, redesign or stop that path rather than add features to make the concept appear more complete.

## How scholarship support would reduce uncertainty

I would use scholarship support to obtain evidence through structured customer validation, prototype materials and test equipment for focused fit, fluid and cleaning questions, and manufacturing and cost investigation. I am not assuming a funding amount, technical result, partnership or commercial outcome.

I use AI extensively to accelerate implementation, research, alternative generation and engineering exploration. I retain responsibility for direction, requirements, priorities, trade-offs and final decisions. I also expect specialist human expertise and collaboration to matter for hardware, manufacturing, IP, fundraising and market entry.
