# Scholarship next-stage evidence plan

[Scholarship review snapshot](SCHOLARSHIP_REVIEW_SNAPSHOT.md) · [Venture progress](VENTURE_PROGRESS.md) · [Customer discovery](CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md) · [Evidence guide](EVIDENCE_GUIDE.md)

Masck One is currently much stronger in inspectable digital development than in customer, physical or commercial validation. My next stage is designed to close that gap before the broader wearable-and-dock vision is allowed to drive additional scope.

This is an evidence plan, not a claim that the work below has already been completed.

## Evidence readiness at a glance

A planned test is not evidence, and digital engineering cannot close a physical or commercial gate.

| Gate | Evidence available now | Status | What closes the gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem significance | I conducted an informal survey of around 20 people. The strongest reported interest was around time/convenience, with concerns around comfort, maintenance and price. The method and raw data are unavailable. | Preliminary only | Structured interviews that establish repeated routine friction, current workarounds and rejection reasons. |
| First-use-case selection | I have explored multiple product and subsystem directions digitally, but I have not locked a first product. | Open | Customer evidence strong enough to select or reject one narrow use-case hypothesis. |
| Wearable fit/workflow | I have digital geometry, requirements and analysis. | Physical evidence required | A focused physical fit prototype and recorded wearing/workflow observations. |
| Fluid behaviour | I have digital fluid-system work and requirements. | Physical evidence required | Controlled, repeatable core delivery and recovery tests for the selected use case. |
| Hygiene/cleaning practicality | I recognise cleaning burden and hygiene as unresolved risks. | Open | A documented cleaning workflow, burden observations and sanitation investigation. |
| Safety | I have safety-related requirements and digital evidence boundaries, but no product-level physical safety evidence. | Physical evidence required | Hazard-specific validation and appropriate specialist review against the narrowed physical configuration. I will not infer safety from requirements, CAD, simulation or passing software checks. |
| Commercial plausibility | I recognise cost and price as uncertainties and do not claim viable unit economics. | Open | An initial sourced manufacturing-cost model considered alongside stronger customer-value evidence. |

The immediate priority is not to make the full concept look complete. It is to move these gates from assumptions to evidence in an order that avoids unnecessary hardware spend.

## Decision-gated next stage

I will not treat the six activities below as a checklist that must be completed regardless of what I learn. Each stage should earn the next one.

| Stage | Work I plan to do | Decision I need before progressing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Establish the problem | Structured customer interviews focused on recent routines, repeated friction, current workarounds, reasons not to change behaviour and price sensitivity. | I need a repeated problem and workflow pattern strong enough to nominate one first-use-case candidate. If it does not emerge, I will narrow, change or reject the use case before expanding physical work. |
| 2. Bound the technical question | Focused simulation and analysis of the highest-risk assumptions for that candidate. | I need to know which assumptions can be rejected digitally and which genuinely require measurement. This keeps physical testing narrow. |
| 3. Test the minimum wearable interaction | A focused physical fit prototype with recorded fit and workflow observations. | I need evidence that the basic wearing, fitting and removal interaction is credible enough to justify wet-system testing. If it is not, I will redesign or stop that path. |
| 4. Test core fluid behaviour | Controlled core fluid-delivery and recovery testing for the selected use case. | I need repeatable physical observations showing whether the minimum fluid interaction is credible enough to continue. This is not product-level validation. |
| 5. Test ownership burden | Hygiene and cleaning investigation tied to the actual first-use workflow. | I need to know whether preparation, cleaning and maintenance erase the intended convenience benefit or require architectural change. |
| 6. Test commercial plausibility | Initial manufacturing-cost modelling using sourced component, process and assembly assumptions, considered alongside customer-value evidence. | I need a first evidence-based view of whether likely scope, cost and customer value can plausibly overlap. If they cannot, I will reduce scope, redesign or stop before broader development. |

A negative result is useful evidence. If an earlier gate fails, I should not spend scholarship support making a later-stage concept appear more complete.

## What I will record from the next stage

I want each activity to leave a reviewable evidence trail rather than only a conclusion.

| Evidence stream | Minimum reviewable record |
| --- | --- |
| Customer interviews | Interview records, recruitment/context notes and a synthesis separating repeated patterns from isolated comments. |
| Simulation and analysis | Assumptions, inputs, method, limitations and the decision the analysis informs. |
| Physical fit work | Prototype purpose, test question, observations and any measured fit/workflow results, without generalising beyond the tested conditions. |
| Fluid testing | Test setup, conditions, measurements, repeats and failures for the narrow delivery/recovery question. |
| Hygiene and cleaning | Cleaning workflow, burden observations, unresolved sanitation questions and any specialist input used. |
| Manufacturing-cost modelling | Sources, component/process assumptions, ranges or uncertainty where appropriate, and the cost drivers most likely to change the decision. |

This record structure matters because I want later claims to be traceable to evidence. It also gives me a clear basis for changing direction when results do not support an assumption.

## Long-term vision versus first use case

My long-term direction is a wearable-and-dock system that could automate selected parts of a skincare routine. I do not treat that vision as a committed first-product specification.

A narrower use case should earn focused physical development only when customer evidence identifies a repeated problem and the required fit, fluid, cleaning and cost questions can be tested without building the complete vision. Weak evidence should narrow or change the concept rather than trigger more features.

## Assumptions that could change the venture

| Assumption I need to test | Why it matters | Possible decision if unsupported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A repeated skincare task creates enough time or handling friction to justify automation. | Without a meaningful problem, technical feasibility has little commercial value. | Reject or redefine the first-use-case hypothesis. |
| A wearable creates net convenience after fitting, loading, removal and cleaning are counted. | Automation fails commercially if servicing burden replaces the effort it removes. | Simplify the interaction, change the form factor or stop the wearable path. |
| Core fluid delivery and recovery can be practical for the selected use case. | Digital work cannot prove real wet-system behaviour. | Redesign or reject the fluid approach before adding functions. |
| Hygiene and cleaning can be managed without disproportionate user burden. | A functioning wet system may still be unattractive or impractical to own. | Change the servicing architecture, reduce wet-system scope or stop the path. |
| Product complexity can plausibly fit the value customers perceive. | A desirable concept can still fail if manufacturing and ownership costs are structurally too high. | Reduce scope, change architecture or reconsider the commercial model. |

## How scholarship support would convert into evidence

Scholarship support would be most useful where it replaces assumptions with reviewable evidence.

| Support area | Evidence it could enable |
| --- | --- |
| Structured customer validation | Stronger interview records and a better basis for choosing the first use case. |
| Prototype materials and test equipment | Focused fit, fluid and cleaning measurements against defined questions. |
| Manufacturing and cost investigation | Sourced process, component and assembly assumptions for an initial cost model. |
| Specialist human collaboration | Practical challenge to my assumptions and AI-assisted work in hardware, manufacturing, IP, fundraising and market entry. |

I do not assume a funding amount, physical result or commercial outcome. A more polished presentation or a more complete-looking prototype is not evidence by itself.

## Current customer evidence boundary

I conducted an informal survey of around 20 people. The strongest reported interest was around time and convenience, while comfort, maintenance and price were concerns. The underlying questionnaire, recruitment method and raw response data are not available in the repository, so I treat this as preliminary and non-conclusive. It does not establish demand, willingness to pay or a validated customer segment.

## My role and AI responsibility

I use AI extensively to accelerate implementation, research, alternative generation and exploration. I retain responsibility for product direction, requirements, priorities, trade-offs and final decisions.

I also expect to need specialist human expertise and collaboration where digital work is insufficient, particularly in hardware, manufacturing, IP, fundraising and market entry. I do not treat AI output, favourable analysis or a passing software check as physical or commercial validation.

## What success at the next stage means

Success is not completing all features in the long-term concept. It is reaching a defensible decision about whether one first use case deserves further development, supported by stronger customer evidence, focused physical measurements and an initial commercial reality check.
