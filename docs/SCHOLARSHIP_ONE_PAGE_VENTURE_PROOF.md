# Masck One: one-page venture proof

> Reviewer summary. This page is deliberately non-technical and evidence-bounded. For the full evidence path, see [Scholarship review snapshot](SCHOLARSHIP_REVIEW_SNAPSHOT.md).

## What is being explored

Masck One is an early-stage, pre-commercialisation venture exploring whether selected repetitive parts of a facial-skincare routine can be automated in a way that creates **net convenience**, rather than replacing routine effort with setup, cleaning or maintenance effort.

The current target-user hypothesis is people who already follow multi-step facial-skincare routines and value reducing active time and repetitive handling. This segment is not yet validated.

## Vision versus first test

The long-term direction is a wearable-and-dock system that could automate selected parts of a routine. That is a direction to investigate, not a committed first-product specification and not a claim about a working product.

The first use case is intentionally not locked. It should be selected only after structured customer interviews identify a repeated problem worth solving. A surviving use case must then earn further development through focused fit, fluid, cleaning and cost evidence.

## What has actually been built

The repository contains inspectable digital engineering work: controlled requirements, parametric geometry, automated checks, source binding, documented design decisions, unresolved-risk tracking and cross-subsystem integration work. This demonstrates active development and an organised engineering process. It does **not** demonstrate product-level physical validation.

An informal survey I conducted of around 20 people provides a preliminary, non-conclusive signal. Interest was strongest around time and convenience, while comfort, maintenance and price appeared as concerns. The repository does not contain the method or raw data, so this is not evidence of demand, willingness to pay or product-market fit.

## What remains uncertain

The decision-critical unknowns are customer problem strength, whether a wearable creates net convenience, population fit and comfort, real fluid behaviour, hygiene and cleaning burden, manufacturing feasibility, cost and willingness to pay. Revenue, partnerships and product-level physical performance are not established.

## What happens next

1. Run structured, behaviour-first customer interviews and select, narrow, change or reject a first use case.
2. Use focused simulation and analysis to reduce high-risk uncertainty before hardware spend.
3. Build a focused physical fit and workflow prototype for the surviving use case.
4. Run repeatable core fluid-delivery tests, retaining failed as well as successful runs.
5. Investigate hygiene, cleaning and servicing burden as part of the convenience proposition.
6. Build an initial manufacturing-cost model from sourced assumptions and compare it with customer-value evidence.

The broader product vision does not override a failed evidence gate.

## What scholarship support would change

Support would be directed towards evidence generation, not presentation: prototype materials and test equipment, manufacturing and cost investigation, structured customer validation, and access to specialist human expertise. No budget is implied until the earlier evidence gates determine what should actually be tested or purchased.

## Founder, AI and collaboration

AI is used extensively to accelerate implementation and exploration. I retain responsibility for direction, requirements, priorities, trade-offs and final decisions. The repository is evidence of my execution with substantial AI assistance, not unaided authorship.

Specialist human expertise remains important for hardware, manufacturing, IP, fundraising and market entry. The next stage should use that expertise to challenge assumptions and improve high-consequence decisions.
