This page defines how future structured customer interviews should be combined into a venture decision. It is a template, not a record of completed research. No entries below should be treated as customer evidence until they are supported by completed interview records.

## Why this exists

Masck One currently has only preliminary, reported by me feedback from an informal survey of around 20 people. That signal suggests interest around time and convenience and concerns around comfort, maintenance and price, but the underlying method and raw data are not available in the repository. It therefore does not establish demand, willingness to pay or a validated customer segment.

The next customer-research step should make contradictory evidence as visible as positive evidence and should decide whether one narrow routine problem deserves further development before broader hardware scope is justified.

## Evidence to aggregate

For each completed interview, record only evidence traceable to the corresponding de-identified interview record.

| Question | Supporting cases | Contradictory cases | What the evidence may support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is the routine problem repeated and important? | Not yet recorded | Not yet recorded | Whether a candidate problem deserves further testing |
| What do people do today instead? | Not yet recorded | Not yet recorded | Existing workarounds and switching friction |
| Is active time or repetitive handling the real burden? | Not yet recorded | Not yet recorded | Whether the current convenience hypothesis is correctly framed |
| Would wearing, loading, cleaning or servicing erase the benefit? | Not yet recorded | Not yet recorded | Rejection conditions for a wearable approach |
| Which narrow use case, if any, is worth testing physically? | Not yet recorded | Not yet recorded | Scope for the first focused fit and workflow prototype |
| What price expectations or constraints appear? | Not yet recorded | Not yet recorded | Questions for later willingness-to-pay and cost work, not a price conclusion |

Do not convert interview count, expressions of interest or positive solution reactions into traction. Recent behaviour, repeated friction, existing workarounds, rejection conditions and contradictory cases should carry more weight than hypothetical enthusiasm.

## Decision gate

After a meaningful set of structured interviews has been recorded, choose one outcome and cite the evidence that supports it.

### Advance

Use only if the evidence shows a repeated problem that matters to a plausible target user, current workarounds leave meaningful friction, and one narrow use case is specific enough to test without assuming the broader wearable-and-dock vision is correct.

### Narrow or change

Use if a real problem appears but the proposed use case, target user or wearable approach does not match the strongest evidence. Record what changes and which observations caused the change.

### Reject

Use if the problem is weak, uncommon, already solved well enough, or the likely setup, wearing, cleaning, maintenance or price burden appears to erase the intended value. Rejection is useful evidence because it prevents further investment in an unsupported use case.

### Inconclusive

Use if the evidence is too sparse, contradictory or poorly targeted to support a decision. Do not resolve uncertainty by counting repository activity as customer validation.

## Handoff to physical and commercial work

Customer evidence does not validate fit, fluid behaviour, hygiene, safety, manufacturability or economics. If a first use case advances, the next work remains evidence-gated: focused simulation and analysis, a focused physical fit and workflow prototype, repeatable core fluid-delivery testing, hygiene and cleaning investigation, and initial sourced manufacturing-cost modelling.

The long-term wearable-and-dock concept remains a direction to test. It should gain scope only when customer, physical and commercial evidence supports the relevant functions.
