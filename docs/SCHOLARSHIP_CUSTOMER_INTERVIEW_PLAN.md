# Customer interview evidence plan

[Scholarship review snapshot](SCHOLARSHIP_REVIEW_SNAPSHOT.md) · [Progress to evidence](SCHOLARSHIP_PROGRESS_TO_EVIDENCE.md) · [Customer discovery](CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md) · [Next-stage evidence plan](SCHOLARSHIP_NEXT_STAGE.md)

Masck One needs stronger customer evidence before a first product is selected. The founder-reported informal survey of around 20 people is useful for forming questions, but its method and raw data are unavailable and it does not establish demand, willingness to pay or a validated customer segment.

This plan defines the next customer-discovery output without assuming that interviews will support the concept.

## Question to answer

Is there one repeated facial-skincare routine problem that people experience strongly enough for a narrow Masck One use case to deserve focused physical testing?

The long-term wearable-and-dock direction is not the interview proposition to validate. Interviews should first test the underlying problem, current behaviour and ownership burden before asking participants to react to a solution.

## Interview structure

### 1. Recent behaviour

Ask for a recent, specific routine rather than general opinions.

- Walk through the last time you completed your normal facial-skincare routine.
- Which steps required the most active time or attention?
- Which parts, if any, were inconvenient enough that you shortened, delayed or skipped them?
- How often does that happen?

### 2. Existing workarounds

- What do you currently do to make the routine faster or easier?
- Have you bought, stopped using or avoided any product because setup, cleaning, comfort or maintenance was inconvenient?
- What would make you keep using a device or tool after the novelty wore off?

### 3. Rejection conditions

The preliminary informal feedback makes comfort, maintenance and price useful areas to investigate, not conclusions to confirm.

- What amount of fitting, loading or preparation would make a routine tool feel like more work than it saves?
- What cleaning or maintenance would make you stop using it?
- What would make a face-worn product unacceptable even if it saved time?
- When deciding whether a skincare device is worth paying for, what do you compare it with?

### 4. Solution reaction, only after behaviour

After the problem discussion, describe only the narrow automation hypothesis being considered. Do not present the complete long-term vision as a finished product.

Record what the participant finds useful, confusing or unacceptable, what they believe it would replace, and why they would or would not change their existing behaviour. Interest in the idea alone is not counted as demand.

## Evidence to capture

For each interview, retain a consistent de-identified record of the participant context relevant to the routine, recent examples, frequency of the problem, current workaround, consequence of the problem, rejection conditions and any solution reaction. Separate direct observations from founder interpretation.

The synthesis should report recurring behaviours and objections, contradictory evidence and cases where no meaningful problem exists. It should not convert interview count or positive reactions into a traction claim.

## First-use-case decision

Customer evidence should feed the existing first-use-case gate in `SCHOLARSHIP_PROGRESS_TO_EVIDENCE.md`. The output is one of three decisions:

1. **Advance:** a repeated problem is supported strongly enough to justify focused analysis and a narrow physical test.
2. **Narrow or change:** a problem appears real, but the proposed interaction, burden or scope does not fit the evidence.
3. **Reject:** the problem is weak, infrequent, already well solved or does not justify the ownership burden being explored.

A positive decision still does not establish product-market fit, willingness to pay or physical feasibility. It only earns the next evidence step.

## Why scholarship support matters here

Structured customer validation can improve the quality of the first-use-case decision before more hardware spend. The purpose is not to produce favourable quotes or marketing material. It is to expose whether the problem is important enough, and whether comfort, maintenance, price or another constraint invalidates the proposed path early.

AI can assist with organising notes, comparing themes and testing alternative interpretations. Founder judgement remains responsible for interview design, evidence interpretation, product direction and the final advance, change or reject decision. Specialist human input is valuable where research design, market interpretation or later commercial decisions exceed the founder's current expertise.
