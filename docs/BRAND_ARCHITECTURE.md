# MASCK brand and product architecture

## Purpose

Masck One is the founding hero product, not the permanent boundary of the company. The parent brand is **MASCK**, intended to support a future premium automated personal-care hardware company while execution remains narrowly focused on making Masck One exceptional.

The required hierarchy is:

- **MASCK**: company/master brand.
- **M-Cut**: standalone master symbol.
- **Masck One**: founding product name.
- **M/1**: compact product designation only.

This prevents the first product from becoming the corporate identity in the way a literal product-name company can become trapped by its original form factor.

## Strategic rule

MASCK is broad in identity and narrow in execution.

The brand can eventually expand into adjacent personal-care hardware only when the founding product has earned customer trust, manufacturing capability and a credible reusable technical/design platform. The repository must not create a speculative portfolio to make the brand appear larger.

## What MASCK should stand for

The working territory is **premium automated personal-care hardware**.

The recurring promise is to turn repetitive routines into precise, low-attention automated experiences. Across products, the strongest reusable signatures should be:

- hidden complexity;
- quiet mechanical precision;
- low active user attention;
- self-location and guidance rather than fiddly setup;
- calm industrial design;
- tactile mechanisms with little play, scrape or rattle;
- restrained light and sound;
- honest physical evidence boundaries;
- engineering depth that is felt rather than visually advertised.

## Master mark: M-Cut

The selected direction is an M silhouette with a deliberate central negative incision. The incision is the recognizable brand geometry. It is **not** a literal numeral one, allowing the mark to remain valid for future products.

The exact vector remains provisional. It must eventually survive:

- 3 to 5 mm physical use;
- one-colour print;
- laser marking;
- emboss/deboss;
- molded microtexture;
- optical inlay;
- app-icon use;
- trademark and confusion review.

The mark must not drift into a literal mask, eye, droplet, leaf, sparkle, infinity sign, power button, medical symbol or generic futuristic slash.

## M/1 relationship

M/1 is derived from the M-Cut system but belongs to the **product**, not the company. It can appear on packaging, device identity surfaces, dock/service labeling and the app. The primary signature control should use M-Cut, not M/1.

## Primary control as brand signature

The strongest current direction is for the primary control to become a recurring MASCK interaction.

The user should feel:

1. almost no initial looseness;
2. controlled resistance build;
3. a crisp, quiet mechanical transition;
4. progressive cushioned travel after the transition;
5. a dense end condition rather than shell impact;
6. a controlled, straight return.

The user should hear a short, muted mechanical sound with little resonant tail. Premium feel should come from guidance, force-path architecture, damping and tolerance discipline, not from deliberately loud sound.

The button architecture must separate functions where beneficial:

- guidance;
- anti-rock/anti-rotation;
- sealing;
- tactile generation;
- sensing;
- landing;
- abuse stop;
- acoustics;
- optical identity.

A purchased tactile switch must not be allowed to define the whole physical experience by default.

## Optical behavior

The optical system should be restrained and integrated into the master mark where practical.

At rest, M-Cut should be nearly tone-on-tone. On wake, the central cut may appear to illuminate from within. CLEAN, MASSAGE, WARM and COOL should avoid arbitrary RGB conventions; white-point shifts and brief acknowledgement behavior are preferred over decorative rings or saturated icon colours.

Exact colour temperature, optical stack and brightness remain hardware-validation items.

## CMF direction

Primary candidate palette:

| Role | Name | Hex | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rigid shell | Mineral Ivory | `#E9E5DC` | Warm mineral, not appliance white |
| Primary control | Warm Porcelain | `#DED9CF` | Slight local distinction, still quiet |
| Facial interface | Soft Stone | `#CFC8BC` | Softer human-contact read |
| Recessive mechanics | Smoke Graphite | `#454542` | Warm dark grey, not black tech paneling |
| Optical inlay | Opal Neutral | `#F2EFE7` | Near tone-on-tone when unlit |

Material grades remain provisional. The selected family should be justified by stiffness, dimensional stability, skin/cleaning exposure, friction, fatigue, acoustic damping, finish and manufacturing process rather than visual appearance alone.

## Exterior language

The product should read as a continuous sculpted personal-care appliance rather than goggles with extra face coverage.

Reject:

- raised eye rings;
- goggle architecture;
- VR/headset cues;
- respirator language;
- medical PPE character;
- tactical panels;
- fake vents;
- robot-face styling;
- arbitrary technical seams.

The eye openings belong to the same continuous facial surface as the cheek and forehead fields.

## Cost discipline

Premium does not mean maximum part count.

Every custom part, insert, elastomer, optical component and precision operation should answer a real need in feel, sealing, guidance, service, durability or identity. If two architectures produce essentially the same user experience, prefer the one with fewer parts, fewer manual alignments, lower calibration burden and more robust process tolerance.

## Repository implementation

The source hierarchy is:

- engineering geometry/performance authority: `config/masck_one_authority.yaml`;
- brand/product identity contract: `config/masck_brand_authority.yaml`;
- brand schema: `schemas/masck_brand_authority.schema.json`;
- brand source assets: `brand/assets/`;
- machine consumer: `src/masck_one/brand_identity.py`;
- generated source-bound identity manifest: `generated/brand_identity.json` through normal release export.

Brand identity can constrain appearance, interaction language, naming and cost priorities. It cannot override protected anatomy, physical safety, wet/dry separation, thermal/electrical limits, geometry truth or validation gates.

## Evidence boundary

The M-Cut geometry remains a design candidate, not a registered or legally cleared mark. CMF values are not production colour capability. The tactile force curve, acoustic quality, ingress behavior, fatigue, wear and subjective premium feel require physical prototypes and appropriate bench validation before they can be treated as product facts.
