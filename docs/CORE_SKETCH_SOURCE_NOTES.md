# MASCK ONE Core Sketch Source Notes

Status: **research-source boundary**  
Date: 2026-09-10

The Core Sketch incorporates conclusions from the 2026-09-10 deep-research session and the earlier product discussion. This file records how those sources may be used without confusing research with product proof.

## Research categories consulted

- dermatology guidance on gentle cleansing and routine ordering;
- peer-reviewed work comparing sonic/mechanical and manual cleansing effects on hydration/TEWL;
- sunscreen application literature discussing standardized test/application density and common underapplication;
- Australian TGA and US FDA sunscreen-use/regulatory guidance;
- public specifications for premium LED/red-light face devices including CurrentBody as a benchmark;
- premium beauty-device, wearable, tactile-control, docking and wet-use product precedents.

## Important interpretation rules

### Cleansing literature

A small study or a competitor sonic-cleansing result does not prove Masck’s cleansing method preserves the barrier. It only supports the feasibility of testing controlled mechanical cleansing without assuming damage is inevitable. Masck requires its own evidence.

### Sunscreen literature

Common sunscreen test/application densities around 2 mg/cm² are a research reference, not evidence that Masck achieves any SPF. Automated application must be validated by the final film and relevant claim/regulatory method.

### CurrentBody / competitor LED specifications

Public wavelength/count/irradiance/session information is a **market benchmark only**. It does not imply:

- open-source hardware;
- permission to clone industrial design;
- that competitor LED count is optimal;
- that MASCK should use a flexible silicone architecture;
- that the same optical claims transfer to Masck.

### Premium-product precedents

Other products may inform perceived precision, low-noise operation, docking choreography, surface quality and interface restraint. They are references for **qualities**, not templates to copy.

## Source-verification rule

Before a research statement becomes:

- an engineering requirement;
- a regulated claim;
- a clinical/safety claim;
- a supplier specification;
- public website copy;

re-fetch the current primary/authoritative source and record the exact evidence in the appropriate engineering/regulatory document.

The research archive intentionally preserves exploratory wording that should not be cited as final authority.
