# Evidence decision gates

I am using the next stage of Masck One to reduce uncertainty, not to make the repository look more complete. These gates state what evidence I need before I increase scope or spending.

| Gate | Evidence I need | Decision I will make |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Problem worth solving | Structured interviews with people who fit my target-user hypothesis, showing a repeated routine problem and the trade-offs they already accept. | I will nominate one first-use-case candidate, narrow the user/problem, or stop that path. |
| 2. Worth testing physically | Focused simulation and analysis that identifies the assumptions most likely to determine whether the nominated use case can work. | I will choose the minimum physical questions to test, redesign before hardware spend, or reject the candidate. |
| 3. Wearable interaction credible | A focused physical fit prototype with direct observations of fit, access and wearing workflow. | I will continue to wet-system testing only if the basic interaction remains credible; otherwise I will change the architecture or scope. |
| 4. Core fluid interaction credible | Physical fluid-delivery testing for the minimum selected use case, with test conditions, measurements and failures recorded. | I will continue, redesign the fluid path, reduce scope or reject the use case. |
| 5. Cleaning does not erase convenience | Hygiene and cleaning investigation tied to the actual loading, use, recovery and maintenance workflow. | I will keep, simplify or reject the workflow based on whether maintenance remains compatible with the convenience proposition. |
| 6. Plausible commercial path | Initial manufacturing-cost modelling using sourced component, material, process and assembly assumptions, considered alongside customer value and price sensitivity. | I will decide whether broader product development is justified, whether scope or architecture must change, or whether the path should stop. |

## What I will record

For customer work, I will preserve the interview structure, participant context at a non-identifying level, recurring patterns and contradictory evidence. For analysis and physical work, I will preserve assumptions, test conditions, measurements, failures and limitations. For cost work, I will preserve sources, quantities, process assumptions and uncertainty rather than presenting an unsupported unit cost.

A gate is not passed because I completed an activity. It is passed only when the resulting evidence is strong enough to support the stated decision. Negative results are useful because they prevent me from spending further time or scholarship support on assumptions that do not survive testing.

## Current position

I have not passed these next-stage gates. My informal survey of around 20 people is preliminary and non-conclusive: the strongest reported interest was around time and convenience, while comfort, maintenance and price were recurring concerns. I do not treat that survey as sufficient customer validation.

My existing repository demonstrates substantial, inspectable digital development, including controlled requirements, code-based CAD, engineering geometry, automated checks, subsystem records and whole-product integration work. It does not establish customer demand, physical performance, comfort, safety, hygiene practicality, manufacturing feasibility or viable unit economics.

The broader wearable-and-dock system remains my long-term direction. I will not treat it as a committed first-product specification until customer, physical and commercial evidence supports a narrower first use case.

## How scholarship support fits

I would use scholarship support to obtain evidence needed by these gates: structured customer validation; prototype materials and test equipment for focused fit, fluid and cleaning questions; and manufacturing and cost investigation. I am not assuming a funding amount or a successful technical or commercial result.

I use AI extensively to accelerate implementation, research, alternative generation and engineering exploration. I retain responsibility for direction, requirements, priorities, trade-offs and final decisions. I also expect specialist human expertise and collaboration to be important for hardware, manufacturing, IP, fundraising and market entry.
