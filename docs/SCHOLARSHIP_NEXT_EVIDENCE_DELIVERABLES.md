# Next evidence deliverables

This page translates Masck One's next-stage plan into concrete evidence a scholarship reviewer should expect to see. These are planned outputs, not completed validation.

The long-term direction remains a wearable-and-dock system that could automate selected parts of a facial-skincare routine. The first product scope is deliberately not locked. Customer evidence should first identify one routine problem worth testing before broader hardware scope is justified.

## Evidence package

| Planned deliverable | What it should contain | Decision it should support | What it must not be presented as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Structured customer-interview record | Interview guide, recruitment notes, de-identified observations, recurring routine friction, current alternatives, rejection reasons and a synthesis of patterns | Select, narrow, change or reject the first use case | Proof of demand, market size or willingness to pay from a small qualitative sample |
| Focused analysis record | Assumptions, model inputs, sensitivity or bounding work, limitations and the specific physical questions that remain | Decide which uncertainties can be reduced digitally and which require hardware evidence | Physical validation or proof that a mechanism will work on people |
| Physical fit evidence record | Prototype purpose, geometry under test, test conditions, measured observations, failures and limitations | Decide whether the selected use case deserves deeper wearable development | Product-level comfort, population fit, safety or production readiness |
| Core fluid-test record | Test setup, fluid conditions, measured delivery/recovery observations, failure modes and repeatability limits | Continue, redesign or reject the fluid approach for the selected use case | Whole-product leakage, hygiene, safety or validated performance beyond the tested setup |
| Hygiene and cleaning record | Preparation, cleaning and servicing workflow, contamination-control questions, observed burden and unresolved risks | Determine whether maintenance undermines the intended convenience benefit | A clinical, regulatory or product-safety conclusion without the required specialist evidence |
| Initial manufacturing-cost model | Sourced component/process assumptions, assembly assumptions, uncertainty ranges and the largest cost drivers | Test whether product complexity could plausibly overlap with customer value | A supplier quote, production commitment, validated unit economics or margin forecast unless those inputs later exist |

## Evidence sequence

Customer interviews come first because the first use case is not yet earned. Focused analysis should then reduce avoidable uncertainty before physical work. Fit, fluid and cleaning evidence should test whether the selected workflow creates net convenience. Manufacturing-cost modelling becomes more useful once a candidate scope is defined tightly enough for credible process and assembly assumptions.

A weak result is still useful evidence. It should narrow, redirect or stop a path rather than trigger more feature development to preserve the original concept.

## Preliminary customer evidence boundary

The repository records a founder-reported informal survey of around 20 people. The strongest reported interest was around time and convenience, while concerns included comfort, maintenance and price. This is preliminary and non-conclusive because the repository does not contain enough underlying method and response data for independent assessment. It does not establish demand or willingness to pay.

## Scholarship support to evidence

Scholarship support would be most useful where it helps produce the deliverables above: structured customer validation; prototype materials and test equipment for focused fit, fluid and cleaning questions; and manufacturing and cost investigation. No funding amount, supplier relationship or validation outcome is assumed.

AI is used extensively to accelerate implementation, research, alternative generation and engineering exploration. Founder judgement owns venture direction, requirements, priorities, trade-offs and final decisions. Specialist human expertise and collaboration remain important for hardware, manufacturing, IP, fundraising and market entry.

The standard for progress is therefore not a more complete-looking concept. It is a better-supported decision about whether Masck One should advance, narrow, change or stop.
