## 7. Mandatory NPI/RFQ questionnaire

### Program and ownership
1. Which parts of this program would be executed in-house, and which would be subcontracted?
2. Who owns CAD, PCB design, firmware, tooling design, test-fixture design and manufacturing documentation created under the engagement?
3. What client files and engineering maturity do you require before DFM, EVT-style build, pilot build and production transfer?
4. How are engineering changes controlled and approved?

### Mechanical and compliant parts
5. What rigid-plastic prototype, bridge-tooling and production molding routes do you recommend for this size class?
6. What silicone/LSR, overmolding, film, gasket and compliant-interface capabilities are in-house versus external?
7. How do you control cosmetic surfaces, sealing interfaces, flash, parting lines, compression features and tolerance stacks?
8. What metrology can be provided for critical sealing and routing geometry?

### Fluidics and hygiene
9. What experience can you evidence with small-pump liquid systems, mixed air/liquid handling, foam, leak containment and drain/dry design?
10. How would you validate prime volume, purge volume, residual volume, retained volume and usable reservoir/cartridge capacity?
11. What fluid-compatibility coupon program would you recommend before material freeze?
12. How do you segregate wet and dry assembly/test operations?
13. What manufacturing controls prevent contamination of wetted paths?

### Electronics and battery
14. What battery-cell and pack suppliers can be proposed with traceability, protection documentation and UN 38.3 test-summary evidence?
15. How are charger/power-supply compliance responsibilities divided?
16. What PCBA inspection coverage is available, including AOI, X-ray, FAI and electrical test?
17. How are firmware flashing, serialisation, calibration and configuration controlled?
18. What environmental and ingress-oriented production tests can be implemented?

### Verification, quality and pilot
19. What DFM/DFA deliverables are issued before tooling release?
20. What constitutes first article approval at your facility?
21. How are CTQs, control plans, incoming inspection, in-process checks and final acceptance defined?
22. What traceability exists from incoming critical components through finished units?
23. What pilot quantity range do you normally use to validate process capability for a product of this complexity?
24. What evidence must exist before you recommend hard tooling or volume ramp?
25. What is your nonconformance, CAPA and supplier-change-notification process?

### Commercial
26. Separate NRE, tooling, fixtures, certification support, prototype units, pilot units and recurring manufacturing charges.
27. State minimum order quantities and minimum annual spend only if applicable.
28. Identify long-lead or allocation-risk components.
29. State payment milestones and cancellation exposure.
30. State what pricing assumptions become invalid if architecture, materials or test coverage changes.

## 9. Prototype and pilot sequence

Stage 1: material and fluid coupons. No integrated-product claim.

Stage 2: isolated fresh-fluid bench. Measure pump envelope fit, pressure/flow sensitivity, dosing repeatability, prime/purge behavior, residual volume, leak behavior and bounded fluid-property sensitivity.

Stage 3: mixed-phase waste bench. Measure air/liquid/foam transport, carryover, retention, cartridge usable capacity, orientation sensitivity, occlusion response, drainage and contamination behavior.

Stage 4: wet/dry packaging mule. Challenge seals, fault containment, service access, cable routing, connectors, drain/dry behavior and fault recovery.

Stage 5: powered integrated alpha. Combine only evidence-supported subsystems. Measure power, thermal, fluid, service, mass/CG and state consistency together.

Stage 6: DFM/EVT-style producer build. Use controlled drawings, defined CTQs and production-representative processes where justified.

Stage 7: pilot. Enter only after first-article, process-control, traceability, test-coverage and supplier gates are closed.

## 10. Producer advancement gates

A producer advances only when all applicable items are evidenced:
- relevant capability is in-house or a named subcontractor is disclosed
- quality-system scope is verified

- IP and tooling ownership are acceptable

- commercial structure is compatible with the current development stage

A polished capability presentation is not evidence of fit.
